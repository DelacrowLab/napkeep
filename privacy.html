Privacy Policy

Last updated: 16 November 2026

Napkeep is a Chrome extension that puts unused tabs to sleep. What it keeps stays on your computer. It has no servers, no analytics and no account.

What it uses

DataWhyWhere it stays
Addresses and titles of your open web pages, their order, window and whether they are pinnedTo decide which tabs can sleep, to show their names, and to keep dated lists of your tabs so you can reopen themOn your computer, in the extension's own storage
Whether you typed on a page (yes or no)So a tab where you are typing is not put to sleepIn that page while it is open, plus a yes/no note in the extension until you leave the page
Your settingsTo remember your choicesOn your computer, in the extension's own storage

To tell whether you typed, the extension notices typing events and the kind of element they happened in (for example, a text field). It does not read what you type, the values of form fields or the text of pages.

It does not send any of this to us, to analytics services or to anyone else. It does not sell data or use it for advertising. Opening a saved page again connects to that website as usual.

Tab lists

Lists of your open web pages (in normal, non-incognito windows) are saved automatically: every list from the last 24 hours, then one per day. Lists older than the period you choose (7, 30 or 90 days) are deleted, including tabs carried over from other lists that day. The clean-up is scheduled every 15 minutes while Chrome is running (Chrome may delay it, for example while your computer is asleep) and also runs as soon as you shorten the period.

Removing your data

Removing the extension deletes everything it stored. Files you exported yourself (JSON, HTML or Markdown) are ordinary files and stay where you saved them.

Limited Use

Napkeep's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. The data is used only to provide the extension's tab sleep, protection and local restore features.

Permissions

Read and change data on websites (http and https) – used to read the address and title of your open web pages (to show them and keep your tab lists on your computer), and to notice whether you typed on a page, including inside embedded frames, right before a tab is put to sleep. Nothing on the page is changed.

Site icons (favicon) – used to show each site's icon next to tab names. The icons come from Chrome's own cache on your computer; nothing is downloaded.
